- Notes: Three short parts and no more. (1) The branches: one paragraph each at most for Sunni, Shia (with the Ismailis as a branch of Shia) and the Khawarij, giving the branch-of, splits-from and opened claims and the one or two early centres, linked to their records. `muhammad.founder-of.islam` is graded C with both sources at stance `reports`; say that Muslims trace the tradition to Muhammad's preaching at Mecca and Medina, not that he founded a school, and do not cite `muhammad.active-at.mecca` (C). `shia.opened` is a span (`0656/0661`) and `ismaili.opened` approximate (`0765~`); give them as such. The Shia paragraph links `[[ali]]`, whose record holds his death and his Mecca and Kufa stays; his claims are not listed here. (2) The four Sunni law schools in one paragraph, linking `[[hanafi]]`, `[[maliki]]`, `[[shafii]]` and `[[hanbali]]`, from eor-madhhab and eor-islamic-law-shariah; only Hanbali has an `opened` claim, so date the others by their first `active-at` claim or not at all. (3) Two pointers: the Sufism collection, through `[[sufism]]` (whose home article is the spoke's landing page) in one or two sentences, and the philosophers who wrote in Arabic, through `[[al-kindi]]`, `[[al-farabi]]`, `[[ibn-sina]]`, `[[al-ghazali]]`, `[[ibn-rushd]]` and `[[theology-of-aristotle]]`, whose home articles are in `platonism/` and are not re-homed here. No claim of those five is listed on this page; their own pages carry them. Keep the page short: it is a map of the collection, and the school records on `/e/` carry the detail until a later corridor writes their pages.
