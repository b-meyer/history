# Wiki plan: islam/sufism

Scope: Spoke collection on Sufism inside the `islam` hub, from the Basra ascetics of the eighth century through the classical masters of Baghdad, Ibn Arabi and Rumi, to the orders and their dated centres up to 1800. Ten pages: the landing page, eight entity articles and one topic page on the tariqa as an institution. Rabia, Hasan al-Basri, al-Qushayri, al-Hujwiri, Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani, Muin al-Din Chishti, Nizam al-Din Awliya, Baha al-Din Naqshband and Ahmad Sirhindi are record-only: the page they belong to names them, links them with `[[id]]` and lists their claims, and none gets a page. The Suhrawardiyya, Shadhiliyya and Safaviyya are record-only on the tariqa page. The six works (mathnawi, tarjuman-al-ashwaq, fusus-al-hikam, futuhat-al-makkiyya, risala-qushayriyya, kashf-al-mahjub) are record-only under their author's page or the landing page.

Folder: `apps/site/src/pages/wiki/islam/sufism/`. The only `entity:` frontmatter under `apps/site/src/pages/wiki/` is in `platonism/`, where al-kindi, al-farabi, ibn-sina, al-ghazali and ibn-rushd have their home articles. This plan links `[[al-ghazali]]` and gives none of those five a page. The `sufism` entity has no home article yet, so the landing page names it in `entity:` (index pages may, `packages/graph/src/wiki.ts:80-96`); the `[[sufism]]` link on `platonism/Al-Ghazali.md` then resolves to this landing page. The landing page is also the `islam` hub's overview of Sufism (`plans/wiki-islam.md`).

Groups run in time order. Inside The Orders, the tariqa page comes first as the frame, then the orders by the date of their first centre: Qadiriyya (Baghdad, 12th century), Chishtiyya (Delhi and Ajmer, 1193), Mevlevi (Konya, 1273 to 1312), Naqshbandiyya (Bukhara, 14th century).

- Sources: eor-junayd (preamble), eor-sufism (preamble: "Origins / Mystical ecstasy", "… / Abū Yazīd al-Bisṭāmī"), hujwiri-kashf-al-mahjub-nicholson (§11.31, §14.3, §14.4)
- Links: [[sufism]], [[al-hallaj]], [[al-hujwiri]], [[kashf-al-mahjub]], [[baghdad]]
- Notes: The page has three own claims; it leans on eor-junayd and on al-Hujwiri's two accounts, the life (§11.31, "ABU ´L-QÁSIM AL-JUNAYD …") and the Junaydis among the sects (§14.4). eor-junayd calls him a major representative of the Baghdad school "associated with its 'sober' and socially responsible trend", and names his uncle Sarī al-Saqaṭī as a doyen of Baghdad Sufism. Al-Hujwiri sets sobriety against intoxication in §14.3 (the Tayfuris, Abu Yazid's followers) and §14.4, writing about 150 years later; cite each for its own view. eor-junayd gives only his death (AH 298/910 CE), so the page has no birth year. Al-Hallaj's relation to Junayd belongs on the al-Hallaj page; here, one sentence and a link.

## Mevlevi

- Title: Mevlevi
- Description: The order of Rumi's disciples at Konya, organised by his son Sultan Walad by 1312, known in Ottoman Istanbul for its turning ceremony, and closed in Turkey in 1925.
- Claims: mevlevi.branch-of.sufism, mevlevi.opened, mevlevi.active-at.konya, mevlevi.active-at.constantinople, mevlevi.closed, mevlevi.influenced-by.mathnawi, mevlevi.influenced-by.shia, rumi.founder-of.mevlevi, rumi.buried-at.konya, sufism.active-at.konya
- Sources: eor-tariqah (preamble: "Individual ṬarĪqahs / Mawlawīyah"), eor-sufism (preamble: "ṢŪfĪ Fraternities / The orders: individual characteristics / Mawlawīyah and Bektāshīyah"), eor-rumi (preamble: "Life / Friends and disciples", "Legacy"), rumi-mesnevi-redhouse (preamble, §3.76, §3.77)
- Links: [[rumi]], [[mathnawi]], [[shia]], [[sufism]], [[konya]], [[constantinople]]
- Notes: `rumi.founder-of.mevlevi` is disputed (see Rumi); this page gives the full account: eor-tariqah and eor-sufism credit Sultan Walad (d. 1312) with organising the order and building the shrine over Rumi's grave, while Redhouse says Rumi "is said to have instituted" it. `mevlevi.opened` (`1273/1312`) spans that. `mevlevi.influenced-by.shia` (A) rests on eor-tariqah's mid-sixteenth-century Shii influences under Dīwānah Muḥammad Chalabī and Yūsuf Sīnachāk; present it as that episode, not as a Shii order. `mevlevi.influenced-by.mathnawi` cites a French passage in Redhouse's introduction (stance `reports`); quote it in French as printed or paraphrase it. The Mevlevi gap: the graph has no dated Mevlevi claim between Konya `1273/1312` and Constantinople `1775/1799`. eor-tariqah describes lodges across Anatolia and a wealthy order with ties to the Ottoman court in those centuries, with no dates the graph holds; the page may say so from the source but must not give a date, a city or a span that no claim supports, and should say plainly that the dated record is thin between 1312 and the late eighteenth century. The map shows no Mevlevi stay through most of that span. The turning ceremony and the European visitors in Istanbul from the late eighteenth century are source-only. The 1925 closing is `mevlevi.closed` (eor-rumi); it falls outside the corridor's span, so end on it briefly.

## Naqshbandiyya

- Title: Naqshbandiyya
- Description: The order named for Baha al-Din Naqshband of Bukhara, who died in 1389, which spread through Timurid Herat to Mughal India, where Ahmad Sirhindi renewed it.
- Claims: naqshbandiyya.branch-of.sufism, naqshbandiyya.active-at.bukhara, naqshbandiyya.active-at.herat, naqshbandiyya.active-at.delhi, baha-al-din-naqshband.born, baha-al-din-naqshband.died, baha-al-din-naqshband.founder-of.naqshbandiyya, baha-al-din-naqshband.active-at.bukhara, baha-al-din-naqshband.member-of.sufism, ahmad-sirhindi.born, ahmad-sirhindi.died, ahmad-sirhindi.member-of.naqshbandiyya, ahmad-sirhindi.influenced-by.ibn-arabi, sufism.active-at.bukhara
- Sources: eor-tariqah (preamble: "Individual ṬarĪqahs / Naqshbandīyah"), eor-islam-central-asia (preamble: "Sufism and Ishanism"), eor-sirhindi (preamble), eor-islam-south-asia (preamble: "Movements of Islamic Renewal and Reform / Early revivalist movements"), eor-sufism (preamble: "ṢŪfĪ Fraternities / Ritual practice / Dhikr"), eor-ibn-al-arabi (preamble: "Continuity and Influences")
- Links: [[baha-al-din-naqshband]], [[ahmad-sirhindi]], [[ibn-arabi]], [[sufism]], [[bukhara]], [[herat]], [[delhi]]
- Notes: Baha al-Din Naqshband and Ahmad Sirhindi are record-only, each in a section of this page. `naqshbandiyya.opened` is held and must not be cited (see Gaps); date the order's start through `baha-al-din-naqshband.born` (1318), `baha-al-din-naqshband.died` (`1388/1389`) and the Bukhara claims. `naqshbandiyya.active-at.herat` (`1404/1490`) is graded C, one source; present the Timurid Herat phase cautiously. The order's India stay is `naqshbandiyya.active-at.delhi` (16XX); no source places the order or Sirhindi at Sirhind, so do not name Sirhind as a centre. `ahmad-sirhindi.influenced-by.ibn-arabi` (B): eor-sirhindi says Sirhindi "belongs to the stream of mystical thought established by Ibn al-ʿArabī, though they differ in certain aspects", and lists unity of being (waḥdat al-wujūd) and unity of appearance (waḥdat al-shuhūd) among his concerns. Present it as that, a debt with differences; the graph holds no critique claim, so do not call him an opponent of Ibn Arabi. Silent dhikr is source-only (eor-sufism "Dhikr", eor-tariqah).
